Abstract

This report benchmarks a commercial retail management software package, Lightspeed Retail (X-Series), against a proposed custom Information System (IS) being developed for Tarlac Truck Parts, a truck parts and accessories retailer in Tarlac City, Philippines. The benchmark package was selected because it combines the capabilities of an inventory-focused specialist and a general small-to-medium business (SMB) point-of-sale (POS) platform. The report describes the features, strengths, and limitations of Lightspeed Retail, summarizes the planned features of the proposed IS, and presents a side-by-side feature comparison in tabular form. A narrative analysis then argues that, although Lightspeed Retail is the more mature and feature-complete platform, the proposed IS is the better fit for Tarlac Truck Parts because of its localization (Bureau of Internal Revenue [BIR] value-added tax [VAT] invoicing, GCash payment support, and Philippine peso pricing), its absence of recurring foreign-currency licensing fees, and its purchasing and analytics features tailored to spare-parts retail.

Benchmarking a Commercial Software Package Against a Proposed Truck Parts Management Information System

Selecting or designing an information system for a retail business benefits from benchmarking—the practice of measuring a proposed solution against an established product to identify feature gaps, strengths, and realistic expectations (Stair & Reynolds, 2020). This report evaluates one widely adopted commercial package, Lightspeed Retail (X-Series), as a benchmark for the Truck Parts Management IS being developed for the client company, Tarlac Truck Parts. Lightspeed Retail was chosen deliberately: it is marketed as an advanced inventory and multi-location platform used by specialty retailers such as hardware, bike, and parts stores, while also serving as a mainstream SMB POS system, so a single package captures both the specialist and the general-purpose perspectives relevant to an auto-parts retailer. The sections below discuss the benchmark’s features and its pros and cons, outline the proposed IS, compare the two in a feature table, and conclude with a recommendation.

The Benchmark Package: Lightspeed Retail (X-Series)

Lightspeed Retail (X-Series) is a cloud-based retail management platform that unifies point of sale, inventory, purchasing, payments, and analytics for small and medium retailers (Lightspeed Commerce, 2026). Its inventory management module provides low-stock alerts, automated restocking, multi-location stock tracking, and bulk updating of minimum and maximum reorder levels across the product catalog (Sehmbi & Crawford, 2026). Its point-of-sale capabilities include cloud transaction processing and barcode scanning through a dedicated mobile scanner application (Lightspeed Commerce, 2026).

For purchasing, Lightspeed Retail allows merchants to generate purchase orders directly from replenishment and inventory reports, and to record landed costs such as discounts, freight charges, and duties on each order for more accurate reconciliation (Lightspeed, n.d.). It also connects to NuORDER, a built-in wholesale catalog of roughly five million products that enables one-click ordering from participating brands (Sehmbi & Crawford, 2026). Its reporting and analytics suite, Lightspeed Insights, offers real-time sales tracking, cross-location trend analysis, and demand forecasting (Lightspeed Commerce, 2026). The platform further supports multi-location management with inter-store stock transfers, integrated card payment processing, customer loyalty and marketing, and e-commerce or omnichannel selling, although the more advanced capabilities are tied to higher-priced subscription tiers (Lightspeed Commerce, 2026; Sehmbi & Crawford, 2026).

Pros of Lightspeed Retail

Independent reviewers consistently praise Lightspeed Retail for its depth of inventory control and reporting, which are included in every subscription tier, and for its strong multi-location and omnichannel support (Sehmbi & Crawford, 2026). The built-in NuORDER wholesale network reduces manual data entry during procurement, and 24/7 chat support is available across all plans. Because the platform is a mature commercial product with an app marketplace and accounting integrations (for example, Xero), it offers an ecosystem that an individual business would not need to build or maintain itself (Lightspeed Commerce, 2026).

Cons of Lightspeed Retail

The most frequently cited drawback is cost. Subscription pricing ranges from USD 109 per month for the Basic plan to USD 339 per month for the Plus plan, with discounts only on annual contracts and additional fees of USD 89 to USD 339 for each extra location or register (Sehmbi & Crawford, 2026). Key capabilities—accounting integrations, advanced reporting, loyalty, and e-commerce—are gated behind higher tiers or paid add-ons, so the effective cost for a fully featured deployment is higher than the entry price suggests. For a single-store Philippine retailer, two further gaps matter: the platform provides no native BIR-compliant VAT invoicing, and its integrated payment processing and e-wallet options are oriented toward North American and European markets rather than local methods such as GCash.

The Proposed Information System

The proposed Truck Parts Management IS is a custom web application (a React front end with a Node.js and Prisma back end) designed specifically for Tarlac Truck Parts. It provides parts inventory management with stock-keeping units (SKUs), categories, stock levels, and low-stock alerting; a sales and POS workflow that generates BIR-style VAT (12%) invoices in Philippine pesos and accepts cash, GCash, cheque, and card payments; and a purchasing module that supports both request-for-quotation (RFQ) and purchase-order (PO) workflows with dedicated supplier management. Its analytics layer produces a daily sales summary, a best-selling parts report with rank-movement tracking, a slow-moving (zero-sales) stock report with last-sale dating, and a low-stock severity report, all exportable as formatted PDFs. Access is governed by role-based permissions for staff. In short, the proposed IS is narrower than Lightspeed Retail but is shaped around the exact operating context of a Philippine spare-parts retailer.

Discussion and Recommendation

Table 1 shows two systems built for different purposes. Lightspeed Retail is the broader and more mature platform: it leads on multi-location operations, omnichannel e-commerce, customer loyalty, forecasting-grade analytics, a large wholesale ordering network, and round-the-clock vendor support. If Tarlac Truck Parts intended to scale into a multi-branch, omnichannel chain tomorrow, Lightspeed Retail would be difficult to beat. For that reason it is an excellent benchmark—it defines the feature ceiling the proposed IS can aspire to over time.

However, the question is which package best suits the client’s current needs, and here the comparison favors the proposed IS. Tarlac Truck Parts is a single-location Philippine retailer that is cost-sensitive and legally required to issue BIR-compliant VAT invoices, and whose customers routinely pay by cash and GCash. Lightspeed Retail addresses none of these local requirements out of the box, yet would impose recurring foreign-currency subscription and per-feature costs for capabilities (multi-location, loyalty, e-commerce) the business does not yet use. The proposed IS, by contrast, delivers exactly the domain-specific workflows that matter to a spare-parts dealer—RFQ and PO purchasing, supplier tracking, and best-selling and slow-moving analyses that drive reorder and markdown decisions—while charging no licensing fee and remaining fully customizable.

The recommendation, therefore, is for Tarlac Truck Parts to adopt the proposed Truck Parts Management IS as its operational system, and to treat Lightspeed Retail as a feature roadmap. Capabilities where Lightspeed clearly leads—multi-location inventory, loyalty and CRM, e-commerce, and more advanced forecasting—should be logged as prioritized future enhancements, so that the custom system grows toward the commercial benchmark without sacrificing the local fit and cost advantages that make it the right choice today.
